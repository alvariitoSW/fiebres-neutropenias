# Judging evidence for a claim

## Verdicts
- **supports** — the study's own finding agrees with the claim, in a comparable population, with the same direction of effect.
- **contradicts** — the study's own finding goes against the claim (opposite direction, or a clear null result in an adequately sized study).
- **partial** — agrees only in part: different population (e.g. HFrEF vs HFpEF), surrogate instead of clinical outcome, subgroup or secondary outcome, observational association for a causal claim, or effect present but not significant.
- **relevant** — question mode only: the article answers the open question; say how in *Finding*, with the number.
- **not found** — nothing relevant with this search. Record the search; it is not proof of absence.

## Choosing the passage
- Copy it **exactly** from the abstract or full text; 1–3 sentences; it must contain the fact you summarise in *Finding* (number, direction, population).
- Take it from methods, results or conclusions — never from the background/introduction, which reports *other* studies.
- For a number, quote the sentence that contains the number.
- If the only support is in a figure or table you cannot read, say so; do not paraphrase it as a quote.

## Primary vs secondary
- **primary**: the study that generated the data.
- **secondary**: review, meta-analysis, guideline, editorial. For meta-analyses this is fine as evidence; for narrative reviews and guidelines, find the original study they cite and offer it to the user — manuscripts should cite the source of the data.

## Design / N
Short: `RCT, n=4041`; `Meta-analysis, 12 RCTs, n=8,900`; `Prospective cohort, n=15,000`; `Case series, n=12`.
